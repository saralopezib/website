01

The seams show

Five product teams, five interpretations of the same interface. Every release meant re-designing components that already existed somewhere else — and customers noticed. Five versions of the same table, found in one afternoon.

I was asked to bring coherence, without slowing anyone down.

The audit wall — 400 screens printed
Close-up — annotating divergence

02

Built from what worked

I printed 400+ screens and mapped every divergence: intentional or accidental. Teams walked the wall with me and marked what they'd fight to keep — the wall stayed up for three months and became a meeting place.

The system wasn't imposed from above. It was assembled from what teams already did well, then given a name, a home, and a way to grow. We shipped the first twelve components with two teams as pilots, and let demand pull the rest.

03

Self-sustaining

Design-to-build time dropped 60%. 120 components live across every product. And the part I'm proudest of — the system became self-sustaining, with every team contributing components back.
